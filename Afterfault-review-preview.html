Why Afterfault exists

Why Afterfault exists

When a part fails early, someone has already paid to fix it. We believe the repair shop that did the work should be able to show the evidence — and recover what the warranty already promised.

Our purpose

Afterfault exists to turn the paperwork after a repair into a clear, evidence-backed warranty case — so service teams stop writing off money they are owed, and suppliers receive claims they can actually assess.

Why it matters

Repair reports, parts invoices and supplier terms almost never sit in the same place. By the time someone pieces them together, the claim window has closed or the evidence is too thin to send. The cost quietly becomes the repairer’s.

The stakes

Warranty is a multi-billion-dollar promise.

Figures reported by Warranty Week from company filings, linked above. They describe the scale of warranty spending, not Afterfault results. Afterfault prepares claims for people to review; it does not decide coverage.

What changes

From scattered paperwork to a claim worth sending.

Today

  • The technician’s report lives in one system, the parts invoice in another.
  • Supplier warranty terms sit in a PDF nobody has open.
  • Claim windows close while evidence is being chased.
  • Small claims are written off because they take too long.
  • Nobody can see which repairs might have been recoverable.

With Afterfault

  • Repair, part and invoice evidence linked into one case file.
  • Each case checked against the supplier’s own terms, with the clause shown.
  • Deadlines and missing evidence surfaced while there is still time.
  • Review-ready claim drafts for an analyst to approve.
  • A clear view of what was claimed, recovered — and why.

SAFEGUARDS
AI assists.
Rules check. People decide.
The prototype already enforces these review gates. Hosting and security items describe the proposed production design.
People decide
Afterfault never submits a claim. Analysts approve, submit through the supplier’s own channel, and record the outcome. Larger claims can require a second approver.
Rules stay rules
Coverage periods, filing windows, labour cover, exclusions, evidence and serial and invoice checks are deterministic rules tied to the supplier’s terms.
AI is labelled and quoted
AI only suggests where a clause exclusion might apply, quoting the technician’s notes and the clause. An analyst must confirm or dismiss it.
No guaranteed recovery
Potential amounts are indications under supplied terms. Eligibility, supplier approval and payment are never guaranteed, and the product says so.
Need-to-know access
Technicians see only the records requested from them. Administrators see totals, not case contents.
Traceable
Every request, record, decision and term change is written to an audit log that can be exported.
What we believe
The rules we build by.
  1. 01
    Evidence first
    Every suggested claim shows the repair record, the part, the invoice and the clause it relies on.
  2. 02
    People decide
    Afterfault prepares. An analyst reviews and approves every claim before it reaches a supplier.
  3. 03
    Fair to both sides
    Clear, well-evidenced claims are easier for suppliers to assess. Good claims help everyone.
  4. 04
    No invented coverage
    If the terms don’t support a claim, Afterfault says so — it never stretches a clause to fit.
  5. 05
    Built for the shop floor
    Technicians record what they found once; the case file does the rest.
  6. 06
    Your records stay yours
    In the proposed design, repair and supplier data is encrypted, scoped to your team and never used to train models.

FAQ
Questions service teams ask.
Does Afterfault submit claims to suppliers?
No. It assesses repairs against the supplier terms you supply, assembles the evidence and drafts a claim pack. Your team approves it, submits it through the supplier’s own process, and records the reference and outcome.
What does the AI do?
In the proposed product, AI reads technicians’ notes against the clause wording and suggests where an exclusion, such as a power surge, might apply. It must quote both, and an analyst confirms or dismisses each suggestion. Dates, amounts, evidence and serial checks are rules, not AI.
Is a “potential recovery” a promise?
No. It is the amount the supplied terms appear to cover, based on the records present. Eligibility, supplier approval and payment are never guaranteed.
What if a record is missing?
The case shows “Needs evidence” or “Eligibility unknown”, which is different from not covered. An analyst can request the specific record from the technician who did the job.
How are labour and parts treated?
Exactly as the terms you enter say: the share of labour covered and any cap. Parts come from the repair report and are checked against the invoice.
Which equipment does it support?
The first focus is commercial refrigeration service. The rule model of coverage, deadlines, evidence and exclusions applies to other serviced equipment.
Is it available today?
Afterfault is in the building stage. The prototype runs in your browser with fictional data; uploads, extraction and AI need the production backend described in the architecture section.
